# Native design prototype

- Start from visual parity with the existing web app under `src/`. Preserve its current layout and copy until Tom requests a design change.
- Tom's first requested redesign uses light mode, menu top left, centred brand mark, search top right, and a floating Liquid Glass tray for Feed, Saved, Queue, and Settings. Home has one horizontally scrolling row of topic pills, starting with All selected; the old feed-order and topic dropdowns are removed from Home. Both the tray background and selection background must be full capsules, with consistent side/bottom spacing. Do not substitute asymmetrical concentric-corner shapes.
- Surprise/random discovery is removed from the visible feed for now. Its future presentation is a bottom sheet; its design and appearance timing are deferred. Do not add an entry point or presentation yet. Horizontal swipes on the topic pills must scroll those pills rather than opening the side menu.
- Reading downward hides the menu/orb/search/topic chrome and slides the bottom tray offscreen; reversing reveals them. Keep the native scroll viewport and content's top inset fixed. One isolated `ReaderChromeMotion` tracks native displacement point for point; local effects translate the header/tray and move the article clipping boundary with the header. Never resize the viewport or change content padding on each scroll sample, and never publish per-point motion into the app shell's state. There is no direction threshold or separately timed slide. Clamp logical tracking through bounce, rebase after layout/data changes, and let a partially visible header accompany short-feed elastic motion. Refresh and returning to the top restore the chrome. Topic-pill hit frames are observation-ignored and read live by the menu recognizer so frame changes do not invalidate the shell.
- Inside the tray, use one equal inset around the selected item. Its darker surface should be strongly frosted. All four icons are outlined until selected, then filled. Home is a minimal rounded house without a door cut-out; Settings uses a simple person silhouette, following Tom's Threads reference.
- The top-left menu reveals a white panel beneath the app by sliding the whole app surface to the right. Preserve the app surface's rounded screen corners and a thin grey outline. Horizontal swipes should track the finger and settle the menu open or closed. Do not present the menu as a sheet.
- The current brand replaces the text wordmark with a code-rendered orb system. Preserve one shared renderer and continuous transitions between the orb, ring, ripple and loading states. Its blue/cyan/violet/pink palette includes white for fades into the page and travelling surges through individual orbs. Keep the single orb's broad falloff; small ring orbs may gain slightly more definition. Never use hard circular masks or canvas boundaries that visibly clip moving orbs. The isolated `--brand-study` canvas is for design review, not product navigation.
- Preserve the approved large single orb. Ring motion should feel calm and coordinated: one broad travelling light, stable orbital positions, minimal scale change, and no competing wobble/squash effects. Smaller orbs may have firmer coloured arcs while their white sides fade fully away.
- Vary contour firmness, arc coverage, direction and falloff independently between smaller orbs and gradually over time. Avoid applying one matching half-soft/half-solid treatment to the whole ring.
- Firmness must morph within a single continuous density falloff. Do not blend in a separate hard-edged circle or thresholded arc mask; that treatment produced visible snapping and was rejected.
- The resting logo is the single orb. Its header treatment is larger; the ten-orb activity ring is slightly smaller. On fresh launch the approved large single orb scales and moves into the home header as opaque frosted white clears over the app. Use one persistent mark for the entrance and resident logo, with no duplicated-logo handoff.
- Pulling unfolds and flutters the ring in proportion to the gesture. Refresh/loading uses the ring, with calm travelling light and growing pigment strength. Success triggers one native completion haptic and gathers back into the single orb. Do not add fast white spins or flashing sweeps. Gate pull state on a real scroll interaction, not layout changes.
- Realistic mock data should populate existing fields and states. Do not add demo labels, preview banners, explanatory placeholders, debug navigation, or invented interface elements.
- Feed posts follow Tom’s Figma design and subsequent refinements: full-width headline, body, then a single-line author/institution byline, then actions. Use the shared 24-point page inset matching the visible menu glyph edge. Headline is Inter Semibold 20/26, body Source Serif 4 (weight 340, optical size 16, default tracking) 16/25; the headline/body/byline gaps are 18 points, with 20 points before the action row, measured from cap-to-baseline layout bounds. Leave 24 points above and below each post divider. Never clip glyphs or truncate headline/body text. Show only the first author and first institution, with muted +X counts for additional entries; only the institution text may ellipsise. Actions use the exact Figma icons at 18 points, heart/discussion/save order, 6 points to counts and 27 between groups. Preserve the exported PNGs: Apple’s SVG importer misrenders the originals’ inside-stroke masks. Likes fill red and saves fill blue. See less and Visit source belong in the top-right three-dot menu, using a 16-point light-grey ellipsis. Counts are separate fixture data, never summary-section counts. The comments area itself awaits design.
- Render the app natively in SwiftUI. Keep design tokens and small reusable components explicit so later visual changes are easy to make.
- Keep backend/API calls and all external side effects out of this prototype. Do not send emails, verify real keys, or access the production database.
- Do not add an Apple signing team, credentials, TestFlight uploads, or GitHub-hosted macOS workflows as part of local design work.
- Build and launch with XcodeBuildMCP on the local Mac. Inspect real simulator screens and verify navigation after changes. The simulator mirror should remain available in Codex while Tom is designing.
- Keep generated builds, screenshots, runtime logs, and machine-local tooling files out of Git. Use the repository's ignored `.data/` directory for local build artifacts.

- Each feed topic has an explicit orb palette; All retains the approved blue/purple field. Category changes recolour only the orb, never the topic buttons. A category transition stays one orb, with a restrained swell/lift/lean; do not enter the loading ring. Keep ring morphing for pull-to-refresh/loading. New pigment must enter through a curved spatial front inside the existing orb, with a gentle luminous wake; do not globally crossfade palette channels. Preserve in-flight fronts on rapid selections, and compact only completed fronts. Rapid category taps must not restart or queue the shape gesture.

- Sift is the working brand. Preserve Tom’s original `Resources/Brand/sift.svg` unchanged. Wordmark placement and usage remain undecided; do not replace the header orb or place the wordmark in product screens until directed.

- Byline markers are the original pale pink 18-point circle for authors and 18-point square with a 2-point corner radius for institutions. The byline row is 18 points high. The illustrated avatar experiments are not displayed.
